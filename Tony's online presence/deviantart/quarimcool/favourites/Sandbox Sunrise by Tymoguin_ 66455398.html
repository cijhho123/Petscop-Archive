Leigh had never seen anything as big and as empty as the desert. It was wasted space where sand was shaped randomly by wind, and an unseen network of roots did battle for every drop of water that had the bad luck to turn up here. Leigh wondered if the water that fell in the desert every truly reappeared anywhere. He knew that it had to, but he could not get over his feeling of doubt as he stared, dwarfed, at the endless desert.


    He could see his objective from where he was, or at least thought he could- the desert heat distorted everything, but yet he knew that it way yet a long way off. With each step he reminded himself that he has one step closer to where he was going. Somehow this helped him make another step after the previous one.


    Leigh wasn't use to this bizarre and troubling emptiness that enveloped him, and his objective was just more of the same. He would reach the airplane eventually and there he would find only sand-polished bones- certainly not something he could talk to, even though he deeply wished he could. He sighed and took his last swig from his canister for a long, long while. He swallowed slowly, savoring the feeling of wetness in his mouth which had grown so alien to him, but not holding it in his mouth long enough so that it became warm. He had thoughtfully left his canister out in the open at night so as to absorb the freezing desert night and carry it forward into the scorching desert day.  He thanked himself for the cool water and, sadly, stashed his canister in his bag, knowing the next time he would be able to take a swig of the water it would be as hot as the desert air. Hopefully it would be nightfall by then and he will have already reached the airplane.


    The airplane belonged to his brother, who he knew briefly but vividly. He remembered the things his brother said to him- all of the things his brother said. He worshiped him and his very words spurred him to action for whatever cause his brother fancied- even today. Even this expedition into the dangerous desert land was his brothers idea, in a way. In a way it was an idea that wasn't really anyone's- it just existed, just like the bonds between two people could exist for no reason at all. Those bonds can be shattered, though their remains can rarely be erased completely. Leigh was doing this for his brother, he was living the life his brother aught to have lived. He was a living statue to his brother's memory. Leigh himself had no life and nothing memorable to be remembered for. Anything   he did was his own doing, but built up on the bravery, the ideas and ideals his brother had and that Leigh was simply using. Nothing he did would have made sense to Leigh had it not been for his brother. Today he was hoping to meet his brother one last time and then, assured of his death, continue finishing his life.


    The sunset and sunrise was a point of transition from one danger to another and, like many dangerous things, it was stunning to behold. Nothing can truly match in beauty a sunset over an empty and desolate desert, except for maybe a sunset over an infinite ocean. Leigh gazed at the sunset until all it's light had disappeared and it became too cold to ignore the night any longer. He settled down for the night, set up a tent, bundled up with all the warm clothes he had and crawled into the tent, planning on sleeping till his starkly out of place digital watch alarm awakened him just in time to stare at the sunrise.


    He couldn't sleep, which stunned him, having walked many miles through a scorching desert. He wasn't exhausted like he was suppose to be. He just was. All that mattered to him right now was seeing his brother. He kicked and turned for a while, eyes closed tightly, but he couldn't go to sleep.


    Instead he got up, packed up and, still bundled in clothing, started walking toward the ruins of the plane, by the surprisingly bright luminescence of the full moon. It didn't take him long to reach the airplane and when he was close enough to correctly comprehend the distance between himself and it he broke into a run and didn't stop until he reached it. He marveled at his own foolishness, thinking that his brother would have never done something like that, just as he saw his brother's skull smiling at him from inside the cockpit, spotlighted by the moon. He was taken aback for a second, stopped in his tracks, but couldn't stop staring at the moonlit skull, trying to find some sort of familiarity in the face of this foreign ThING. Leigh did finally- he saw his brother's gold filling. Suddenly a wave of nausea overtook him and he couldn't keep himself from vomiting all over the white sand dunes. When he finished he just sat, immobile, on his knees, not looking at his brother's skinless face, but being aware of it's looming presence. For the longest time this was all he could do. His brother's skeleton looked almost like any skeleton Leigh had every seen and he would have been fine with that, had it not been for the lingering and subtle individuality. He noticed other similarities now, for he couldn't stop seeing the image in his head, recalling every detail. The skeleton also recalled vague, childlike fears Leigh faced when his brother was still alive. He, like any child was afraid of the unknown and was terrified of death or dead things and expected to find them lurking everywhere in the darkness. It was his brother who helped him get over his little kid phobias, but now those fears where manifesting themselves and with more irony then Leigh could handle at the moment.


    After a while Leigh got up the guts to look at his brother's skeleton face. He looked and was terrified and disgusted. He tried to graft his brother's face, surviving in his memory, onto this hollow skull, but he couldn't see his brother's face. only the skeleton that had eaten it. His brother was making Leigh become himself, someone he hasn't been for 12 years now. Leigh realized this and decided that starting again from a 10 year old's eyes was just too much. He had to be like his brother, but not the one sitting dead and bare in an airplane cockpit. He swallowed, got to his feet and opened the rusted airplane door. He saw the skull wearing his brother's clothes and the bracelet he had made for his birthday one year on his bony wrist. Leigh overflowed with emotions. A combination of disgust, anger, terror and sadness assaulted him. He cried. Cried tears of an emotion no one had named yet. Leigh was torn between the options of giving the skeleton a hug or smashing it's smiling face in. No other options occurred to him. He went with both. The skeleton's torso in his clasp and the skeletons face lying in bits on the cockpit floor, Leigh decided he had gone insane and had to find a place to gather his sanity. He grabbed the bracelet of the skeleton and ran until he couldn't run anymore. Then he collapsed on the ground and thought. Whenever he thought he couldn't escape his skeleton brother's skull lingering in his memory. In the real world Leigh would have found something mindless and hypnotic to stare at- focusing on a funny TV show so he could forget bad things existed at all. But what could he do in this dream world where all there was was sand? He didn't know. He went to sleep then and there. He didn't know how he fell asleep but something likely helped him. When his alarm went of the next morning he didn't wake up and the sun rose without him.
