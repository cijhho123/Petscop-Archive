Chapter One – “Chapter of Logic”
September 2nd, 2008

   The story of logic. That is exactly what this is not. In fact, you could even say that the name ‘Story of Logic’ is an utter lie. This is actually a story of complete nonsense, a story of unintelligence. You will never see nor read any type of ‘logic’ in this story.
   With that said, let us start a new subject. You know the world pretty well, right? You know that it’s a sphere, you know that it moves in all sorts of ways, etc. You know lots of things that people like to tell you. That’s just it, though. People tell you these things, with hope that you will respond with a “Wow!” or an “Awesome!” or something along the lines of those words. They don’t let you do the thinking, they just say, “Nah, we’ll take care of it, kiddo.”
   People go to school. They learn things about life; they learn things about the world around them. If the teacher says that atoms are the smallest objects in the universe, then atoms are the smallest objects in the universe. No further question. We have the technology to find out those things. After all, if we can’t see something, it isn’t there, right?
  There are people who we like to call ‘superstitious’.  Those people are not scientists. Those people are the imaginative ones. Some of the things that they say sound ridiculous, but they believe them nonetheless. The one thing that we don’t understand, though, is that these types of people could be right. Do we have proof that ghosts don’t exist? Do we have proof that the dead couldn’t at any time break out of their graves and kill us all? We actually do not.
   This is uninteresting though; nobody wants to read all of this meaningless babble. People want to read about stupid people, and that’s exactly what they will be reading about. The story of logic will now begin.
   The story starts (and ends) in a miniature town called Phailville, populated by obnoxious losers. Nothing really happens there, but that’s the beauty of it; it’s peaceful. No fights ever go down, nobody dies, nothing unfortunate at all. The citizens, however, balance this peace with stupidity. On one hand, it’s a nice, calm town. On the other hand, the people are awfully annoying. So, it’s a nice town if you aren’t a social person.
    One smart fellow who has to endure these people is a young man named Justin Wilemind. He’s the kind of person who acts like he’s above everybody, and he probably is. You probably are, too. It’s not that hard to feel that way compared to the competition, the competition being the rest of the citizens of Phailville, particularly the 15 and under group of citizens. As I have made apparent, they aren’t very smart people.
     Justin Wilemind is a 14 year old boy with brown hair and unimportant facial features. He tends to be a logical person; he won’t accept any fact without some supporting evidence. He also gets good grades in school for being smarter than everyone else. It wouldn’t be very hard to get good grades in his school though, after a while they lowered the standards for them. Failing is a 40 and under, and getting a 60 or above is considered a great grade. Justin gets 90’s and 100’s.  
     Anyway, it’s 10 o’clock in the morning, and Justin is woken up.  He’s too tired to be able to tell who or what had woken him, but it was most likely his mother who had done it. He rises out of his bed at a speed of about .001 meters per second, and (eventually) makes in out of his room.
   “Ugh, why the crap did you wake me, mom?” Justin says, practically undead. He doesn’t get an answer, so he just walks down some stairs and arrives in the kitchen. His mother is opening the fridge to get something.
   “I’m tired, why am I awake?” he mumbles.
   “Because it’s a nice day out and you’re sleeping. Go outside and do something, for heaven’s sake.” she replies, as she closes the fridge.
   “Like what?”
   “I don’t know, something that’s fun, I guess.”
   “Like what, though?”
   “Think of something.”
   Justin looks to his left and notices the TV is on in the living room. He sits down on the oh so comfortable couch and watches the pictures move. Some guy is on TV talking about something. He looked like a scientist, one of the possible things Justin wanted to become some day in his future. This made him interested, so he started listening to what the scientist had to say.
     “So, I hear you have discovered something that could benefit mankind. What is it, exactly?” some news guy asks, as if he’s interested (which he probably isn’t).
     “I’m currently working on a large project that could stop all types of motion sickness,” the scientist replies, loving himself. “You see, the world is always moving in some way.  It’s turning and traveling, but it is also vibrating. This may not be noticeable, but it indeed changes everyday life. If this vibrating were to stop, there would be no motion on the ground or water. It’s hard to believe, knowing what we already know about the forces of gravity, but I have proven this theory to be correct.”
    Justin just stares at the TV, astonished. He knew much about gravity, he knew much about all kinds of science. What that man had just said disproved a huge load of things that Justin had already learned from other sources. All motion on the ground –stopping? It didn’t make any sense at all.
    His mother then comes into the room and notices that he is still in the house. She gets a sort-of-angry-but-not-quite look on her face, and pretends to talk like she means business.
    “What did I tell you?” she asks.
    “I don’t know, what did you tell me?” Justin responds.
     “It’s almost the end of your summer vacation, tomorrow you’ll be at school again. Make use of this time.”
     “I am making use of it; I’m watching something interesting on TV.”
     “What? Did somebody die on it or something?”
     Justin’s mother never really had much of an attention span.  It doesn’t matter what it is she’s talking about, she’ll eventually drop the conversation in favor of something different. This is usually at her expense, whether she’s aware of that fact or not.
     “No, not on this channel,” Justin says. “I’m sure somebody died on another channel, but not this one.”
     Justin turns around and notices that his mother has a cigarette in her hand. She’s sort of hiding it, but it can easily be seen behind her.
     “You know, I’m going to have to hide those from you some day, and you will not be happy about that.” he says.
     She’s been smoking for years, and Justin has always attempted to stop her, usually with smart, cunning plans that require proper planning. None of them worked for very long, and he pretty much gave up after a while.
     She just walks away after hearing that, practically ignoring what Justin just said. She ignores every comment that she doesn’t enjoy, which is about half of everything she hears. Needless to say, criticism is her number one enemy.
    Suddenly, a painfully earsplitting sound comes from outside. Justin covers his ears and promptly looks out the window to the left of him. He doesn’t see anything, so he gets up and tries to find the source of it. Up in the sky, he sees something falling with forceful speed. The object is way too far away, so he can’t make out exactly what it is. It’s smoldering, as if it had fallen all the way from space.
     Justin’s mother comes over to find out what is going on. “What the heck is making that noise?” she asks with a loud enough voice to be heard from a mile away, covering her ears as well to block out the horrible sensation caused by the deafening sound.
     “It’s that thing, up there.” Justin says, and points out the window to the flaming object falling towards the Earth.
     Then, after the long fall, it crashes into the ground violently. This crash is followed by another painful sound. Justin uncovers his ears and looks closely at the spot that the thing had fallen. He can’t see anything, not even a sign that something had crashed. That’s because it fell far into the woods, where nobody could view it. A long silence occurs. For a while, neither of them says anything; they just stare outside.
     “…mind if I check it out?” Justin asks, still shocked.
     “V-v…very well, then. You go ahead.” his mother replies, with some fear in her voice.
     So Justin goes over to the front door, opens it, walks out of the house, and then closes it. Curious of everyone’s reaction, he looks around at people’s faces. The look pretty darn shocked. He walks up to his neighbor, some old guy named Bill. He looks just as shocked as everyone else.
     “What do you think that thing was…?” Justin asks the old man.
     Bill turns around and looks at him. “Oh, that? That was… nothing. At least nothing worth checking out.” he says.
     “It was more than nothing. In fact, I was just about to find out what it was.” Justin replies.
     “Oh, you don’t want to do that.”
     “Why wouldn’t I?”
     “Don’t you remember the two kids that disappeared there? That area is haunted.”
      Justin just rolls his eyes.  “I’m surprised at you. Sure, they disappeared there, but that doesn’t make a place ‘haunted’.” he says.
     “Whatever, I just know that I’m never going to go over there.” Bill responds.
     Justin looks over to the area where the thing fell. He’s never really been over there before, over that hill, into the woods. Nobody he knew ever went over there. Could that really be the reason? Is it just because of those kids? It didn’t matter; he was still going either way.
     He headed on down the street, walking by the many confused and frightened people. Nobody was going over there to check it out. They were all too scared that they would be the next ones to disappear. He walked by them all, then started going up a hill leading toward to woods. Some people stared at him, wondering why he was actually going to check it out. He stopped about twenty feet away from the woods, and just started to think if this were a good idea. After about a minute, he decided to move on.
     He made it into the woods, at last. It was kind of annoying though, the trees were poking at him, and he came without shoes on so the sticks on the ground really hurt. Especially in the morning, when everything seems to hurt twice as much. He kept walking, and after a while started to smell smoke. He knew he was getting closer to it, so he moved a little bit faster.
    After some more walking, he found a large hole. Not just large though, it was gigantic. He thought for a second that this hole was made from the falling object, but that wouldn’t have made sense at all. The smoke seemed to be coming from it though, so there could be no other explanation.  Unless the hole was created by something else, but what? It didn’t matter to him; he wasn’t in a thinking mood at the moment. He looked into the hole, and what he saw was quite strange. He looked closer.
     The hole seemed to never end, it looked like it just kept going forever. There were stairs leading down the hole, all the way down until they met the darkness. Why would there be stairs? Justin stepped a little closer to get a better view of it. They were stairs, alright. It just didn’t make any sense at all.
     Becoming more interested, he steps even closer. Then closer. Then closer, until he loses his balance and is sent plummeting down the gigantic hole. He missed the stairs. He started to scream at full volume as he fell down, but that wouldn’t help him from splattering on the bottom of the hole, if there even was a bottom. After he fell for about ten seconds, he heard something flying up in his direction. It hit him fast and hard, and started to push him up out of the hole.
     All this time, he was still screaming at the top of his voice. In half the time it took for him to fall down the hole, he’s  sent flying out of the hole at high speeds, then he starts falling back to the ground fast. He falls on his back and shrieks in pain.
     He stays in this position for a long time, trying to figure out what he had just gone through. After giving it a good thinking through, he gets up and starts looking around. He looks left, then right, then forward, then back. Then he looks up and realizes that something’s about to fall on top of him, so he moves a little bit to the right.
     It smashes into the ground, and shakes everything around it. Then, silence. Justin looks at what almost fell on his head, and realizes that it looks like the object that fell from the sky. It also seems to be the same thing that saved him from falling down the hole. It’s very strange looking; it’s shaped like a human, but completely blue all over. Its hands have little diamond-like things on them, as well as its neck. They‘re very shiny; he had to slightly close his eyes while looking at them.
     After remembering how highly impossible this is Justin steps back in fear, and to top it all off, it starts glowing for some reason. At that, he starts to run as the light becomes more and more intense and hurts his eyes.
     The strange creature eventually stops glowing, and suddenly looks quite different. It has gone from a blue human-like thing to a human-like human. In easier to understand words, it becomes a human. A human girl, in fact. One with dark brown hair, and once again, unimportant facial features. She looks ahead and notices the boy running for his life. She looks closer. She notices something different than that now. She notices something… that she needs. Something she needs right away, in fact. Something… in Justin.
     She gets up to chase after him, but quickly falls back down. What made her fall back down? She tries again, but just falls back down to the ground again. Wait a second… gravity. That’s what she had forgotten all about. Gravity had just pushed her toward the Earth, and it will continue to push her. How can she resist falling down? Wait another second… walking. Walking is yet another thing she had forgotten about. How can this be done?
     It was too late. The boy was completely gone from her sight at this point. He had gotten away, and there was no way that she could track him down now.  She tries getting up again, finds her balance, then loses it and falls. Tries again, falls. It will take a lot of practice to get this right. Alright, get up, stand…
